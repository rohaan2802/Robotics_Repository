EULER ANGLES

Three angles that describe the orientation of a body in 3D as a sequence of rotations about coordinate axes, relative to a fixed reference frame. Used in robotics, aerospace and computer graphics.

The three rotations (usually written α, β, γ)
- Yaw (heading)   - rotation about Z (vertical axis), like turning left/right on a rotating platform
- Pitch (elevation) - rotation about Y (side-to-side axis), like tipping the head forward/back
- Roll (bank)     - rotation about X (forward axis), like tilting the head towards a shoulder
The order of rotations depends on the convention used.

Intrinsic vs extrinsic
- Intrinsic: rotate about the body's own axes; the frame moves with the body after each rotation.
- Extrinsic: rotate about the fixed global axes; the frame does not move.

Common sequence: Z-Y-X (yaw, pitch, roll)
1. rotate about Z (yaw)
2. then about the new Y (pitch)
3. then about the new X (roll)

Problems
- Gimbal lock: two rotation axes line up and one DOF is lost, so the body can no longer rotate independently about all three axes.
- Ambiguity: different conventions / rotation orders give different results for the same three angles, so the convention must always be stated.

Where they are used
- Aircraft and spacecraft orientation (roll, pitch, yaw)
- Orientation of a robot end-effector
- Animation in graphics (quaternions often preferred to avoid gimbal lock)

Key point: intuitive way to describe orientation with three sequential rotations, but suffers from gimbal lock and convention ambiguity - quaternions avoid these issues.
